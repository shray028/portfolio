Transfer learning — stand on ImageNet's shoulders

Training a CNN from scratch needs millions of images. A network pretrained on ImageNet already knows edges, textures and object parts — reuse that knowledge, replace the last layer, and train on a few thousand images of your own.

Features are reusable Must-know

Layers of a CNN trained on ImageNet (1.2M images, 1,000 classes) form a hierarchy. The early layers are almost universal; the last layers are specific to ImageNet's classes. So we keep the general part (the backbone) and swap the specific part (the head).

EARLY LAYERSEdges & coloursOriented lines, colour blobs — useful for any image.
MIDDLETextures & patternsFur, grids, stripes, simple shapes.
DEEPObject partsEyes, wheels, petals — more dataset-specific.
HEAD1,000 ImageNet classesThrow away; replace with your own classes.

Concept

Frozen = requires_grad=False Must-know

A frozen layer still runs in the forward pass but gets no gradients and is never updated — so it costs no optimizer memory and its knowledge is preserved. Feature extraction freezes the whole backbone and trains only the new head. Fine-tuning also unfreezes the deepest blocks, with a small learning rate.

Two questions decide how much to unfreeze

How much labelled data do you have? And how similar are your images to ImageNet photos? More data lets you safely update more weights; less similar images need deeper layers to adapt.

A two-phase recipe that works

  1. Load pretrained weights and their matching transforms.
  2. Freeze the backbone, replace the head, train a few epochs (lr ≈ 1e-3).
  3. Unfreeze the last block(s), train a few more epochs with a smaller lr for the backbone (≈ 1e-4).
