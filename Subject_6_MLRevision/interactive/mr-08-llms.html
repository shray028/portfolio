Concept

BPE: start from characters, repeatedly merge the most frequent pair Must-know

Byte-Pair Encoding learns a vocabulary from a corpus: begin with bytes/characters, count adjacent pairs, merge the most frequent into a new token, repeat until the vocabulary hits its size (32k–200k). Common words become single tokens; rare words split into sub-words, so there is never an out-of-vocabulary word. Side effects interviewers like: models see "strawberry" as a few chunks, not letters (hence letter-counting mistakes); numbers split inconsistently (arithmetic errors); non-English text uses more tokens per word (higher cost and shorter effective context). WordPiece (BERT) and SentencePiece/Unigram are close cousins.

Concept

The model gives a distribution; the decoding strategy picks a token Must-know

\( p_i=\frac{e^{z_i/T}}{\sum_j e^{z_j/T}} \)

Temperature T rescales logits: T → 0 approaches greedy (always the top token), T > 1 flattens the distribution (more random). Top-k keeps only the k most likely tokens. Top-p (nucleus) keeps the smallest set whose probabilities sum to p — adaptive: few tokens when the model is confident, many when it isn't. Use low temperature for extraction, classification, code and RAG answers; higher for brainstorming and creative writing. Greedy and beam search are deterministic but can be repetitive.

Concept

Prompting changes the instructions, RAG changes the knowledge, fine-tuning changes the behaviour Must-know

Start with prompt engineering (clear instructions, few-shot examples, structured output) — cheapest and fastest to iterate. Add RAG when answers depend on private, large or frequently changing knowledge, or need citations. Fine-tune when you need a consistent style or format, a specialised skill or vocabulary, or a smaller/cheaper model to match a big one on a narrow task — not to teach facts that change. They combine: a fine-tuned model can still use RAG.

Concept

From next-token predictor to assistant Must-know

A base model trained on web-scale text predicts plausible continuations — it knows a lot but doesn't follow instructions. SFT fine-tunes it on curated instruction→response demonstrations. Preference optimisation then teaches which of two responses humans (or AI judges) prefer: RLHF trains a reward model and optimises the policy with PPO under a KL penalty to stay close to the SFT model; DPO optimises the same preference objective directly as a classification loss on (chosen, rejected) pairs — no reward model or RL loop, simpler and more stable.
