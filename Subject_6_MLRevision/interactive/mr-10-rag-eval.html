Order-unaware vs order-aware metrics Must-know

\( P@k=\frac{\#\text{rel in top }k}{k}\quad R@k=\frac{\#\text{rel in top }k}{\#\text{rel total}}\quad \text{MRR}=\frac1{\text{rank of first rel}}\quad \text{nDCG}@k=\frac{\sum_{i\le k} rel_i/\log_2(i+1)}{\text{ideal DCG}} \)

Recall@k is the key retriever metric for RAG: if the answer isn't in the retrieved set, the LLM can't use it. Precision@k measures noise in the context. Both ignore order. MRR only cares about the first relevant hit (good for single-answer questions). MAP averages precision at every relevant hit (multiple relevant chunks). nDCG discounts relevance by position and supports graded relevance. Click results to mark them relevant.

Concept

Four numbers locate the failure Must-know

Context recall: does the retrieved context contain everything needed for the reference answer? (retriever coverage). Context precision: are the retrieved chunks relevant, and ranked first? (retriever noise). Faithfulness / groundedness: is every claim in the answer supported by the context? (generator hallucination). Answer relevance: does the answer address the question? (generator focus). Frameworks like RAGAS, TruLens and DeepEval compute these with an LLM judge — calibrate the judge against human labels before trusting it.

How to answer these

Localise first: retrieval, generation, data or system? Must-know

Pull the trace for failing queries (query, rewritten query, retrieved chunks with scores, final prompt, answer). If the needed chunk isn't retrieved → retrieval/data problem. If it is retrieved but the answer is wrong → generation/prompt problem. Then fix with the smallest change, add the case to your eval set, and re-run the suite so it never regresses. Concept

Lost in the middle Must-know

Studies of long-context models (Liu et al., 2023, "Lost in the Middle") found accuracy is highest when the relevant passage is at the start or end of the context and drops when it sits in the middle, and that adding more retrieved passages can hurt. Mitigations: retrieve less but better (re-rank, threshold), put the strongest chunks first (and/or last), compress or summarise context, and split multi-part questions.
